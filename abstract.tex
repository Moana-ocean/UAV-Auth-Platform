\begin{abstract}
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) operations in delivery networks require reliable ground-control-station (GCS) authentication before mission or telemetry services are granted. This dissertation designs, implements and experimentally compares two application-layer mechanisms that satisfy the same challenge--response security objective: a conventional X.509 public-key infrastructure baseline and a permissioned-blockchain identity registry deployed on a local four-validator Hyperledger Besu QBFT network (chain ID~20245).

A controlled 53-step workload matrix evaluated security scenarios, scalability over registered identity counts ($n \in \{10,50,100,250,500\}$) and concurrency levels ($c \in \{1,5,10,25,50\}$), and optional on-chain audit logging. After instrumentation hardening (Method~B), the scalability cells were re-executed with ascending-$n$ population growth, sequential warm-up, measured-only throughput timing, and measured repetitions $\max(30,c)$. Supplementary independent ABBA batches added 880 further observations at representative workload points.

Under the hardened evaluation, both mechanisms achieved full normal-authentication acceptance in the adversarial security battery (750/750 measured repetitions per mechanism) with zero false acceptances. On the Method~B scale re-run (\texttt{method\_b\_20260904}), all 1\,700 measured scale decisions were accepted with zero false rejections. At low concurrency ($c=1$, $n=500$), mean accepted decision latency was 2.03~ms (X.509) versus 22.89~ms (blockchain); completed throughput at high concurrency ($n=500$, $c=50$) was 76.98~requests/s versus 38.66~requests/s. Mean runner-process CPU utilisation across scale cells was higher for blockchain (66.95\% versus 50.28\%). Optional immutable audit transactions remain available (prior audit cell: mean confirmation $\approx$ 1.95~s; 30\,291 gas units per audit record).

The results support using X.509 where minimal latency and operational simplicity dominate, and using a permissioned registry where distributed identity status and append-only auditability justify measurable performance cost---provided RPC resilience and validator capacity are engineered for concurrent \texttt{eth\_call} load. All measurements were obtained on a single Windows host with loopback networking; they characterise the implemented prototype rather than wide-area operational deployment.
\end{abstract}
