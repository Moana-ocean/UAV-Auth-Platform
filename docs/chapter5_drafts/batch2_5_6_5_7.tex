\section{Authentication Latency Results}
\label{sec:ch5-latency}

Decision latency is the interval from GCS receipt of a complete authentication request until an accept/reject decision (Section~\ref{sec:ch5-workloads}). Table~\ref{tab:ch5-latency} reports descriptive statistics for \textbf{accepted} requests only (\texttt{latency\_set = accepted\_only}).

At low concurrency ($c=1$), X.509 mean decision latency remained near 1.4--2.1~ms regardless of registry size ($n=10$: 2.13~ms; $n=100$: 1.44~ms; $n=500$: 1.60~ms). At $c=25$, mean latency increased to 40.9~ms ($n=10$), 38.0~ms ($n=100$), and 50.6~ms ($n=500$). At $c=50$ and $n=500$, mean latency reached 41.3~ms (median 48.4~ms, p95 72.9~ms, CI$_{95}$ [33.4, 49.0]~ms).

For the blockchain mechanism at $c=1$, mean accepted latency was 16.8~ms ($n=10$), 21.6~ms ($n=100$), and 18.8~ms ($n=500$), reflecting registry lookup via \texttt{eth\_call} before signature verification. Under higher concurrency ($c=25$--$50$), blockchain mean latencies rose to 180--249~ms as JSON-RPC contention increased on the co-located validator nodes.

\section{Throughput and Failure Rate}
\label{sec:ch5-throughput}

Completed throughput (\texttt{completed\_throughput\_rps}) was computed as finished authentication attempts divided by the measured batch wall-clock interval. Table~\ref{tab:ch5-throughput} lists representative values for \texttt{valid\_active} scalability runs.

X.509 throughput approached 59.6~rps across most configurations (e.g.\ $n=500$, $c=50$: 59.65~rps), consistent with client-side saturation on the eight-core host. Blockchain completed throughput was approximately 29~rps (e.g.\ $n=500$, $c=50$: 29.16~rps), roughly half the X.509 rate under the same threaded client model and co-located Besu validators.
